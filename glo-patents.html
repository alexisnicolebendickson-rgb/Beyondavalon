GLO Patent Map: The Jones Patents, 1814–1833

The key finding on this page: the patent image for Lewellen Jones's accession CV-0109-122 names "Leroy Pope assignee of Lewellen Jones" — the founder of Huntsville took assignment of Jones's claim. That patent, signed by President James Monroe and Josiah Meigs, Commissioner of the General Land Office, is the signature evidence that the Jones family was doing business inside Huntsville's founding elite circle, and it is why the GLO patent sweep was reprioritized to identify which Pope first.

Dataset note: All thirteen patents below were verified October 2, 2026 from BLM General Land Office records, Huntsville Meridian, Madison County, Alabama — the records do not place them in Morgan County. The seven core patents issued November 20, 1818, under the Credit Volume (Harrison Land Act of April 15, 1800), name either Leroy Pope or David Moore as Lewellen Jones's co-patentee.

How to read this map. Each township is drawn as a schematic section plat — a 6×6 grid of numbered sections in the standard PLSS layout (north at top), matching how the original land-survey plats were organized. Shaded quarter-sections mark the patented aliquots; colors group the patents by co-patentee. This is a schematic of the survey grid, not a geographic map — no geographic coordinates or distances are claimed. Click (or tap) any shaded section for the full patent detail: accession number, date, authority, and patentees.

Every patent plotted above, in accession order as recorded. Township abbreviations: T = Township (South), R = Range; Huntsville Meridian throughout. The BLM GLO records list all of these in Madison County, Alabama.

Map data & sources

  • Bureau of Land Management, General Land Office (BLM GLO) — all thirteen patent records and accession numbers (CV-0109-108, CV-0109-122, CV-0109-124 through CV-0109-129, CV-0107-187, CV-0109-462, CV-0121-066, CV-0125-311, STA-AL1210__.163); Huntsville Meridian survey framework. The patent image for CV-0109-122 was read for the "Leroy Pope assignee of Lewellen Jones" language and the Monroe/Meigs signatures.
