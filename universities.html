The Counter-Archive · Comparative Research

Universities Built on Plantations

The University of Alabama in Huntsville is not an exception. This page is The Counter-Archive’s verified comparative list: 62 institutions documented, 44 scored in depth, each one graded separately on five questions — A, was the campus sited on plantation land? B, did enslaved people build or operate it? C, did it benefit financially from slavery? D, is the land disputed? E, what has the institution done about it? Every grade — Documented, Reported, or Not documented — comes from a source actually read, and figures are attributed in line, never upgraded.

The Anchor Case

UAH / Jones Plantation

The list begins at home. The UAH campus sits on the plantation estate land of Alexander P. Jones in Huntsville, Alabama — A: Documented. The 1956 condemnation of Willie & Lola Jones’s homestead parcel ($900 award, acreage contested), the Jones family cemetery behind Morton Hall, and the ground-penetrating radar survey announced in May 2022 whose results were never published make it one of the list’s live disputes — D: Documented.

What this project does not claim: a slavery-derived founding endowment for UAH is not documented, and it is not claimed. The Counter-Archive claims the land, and the knowledge record — what the university knew about the ground it built on, and when it knew it.

The Burial-Ground Census

Fifteen campus burial grounds are on file across the list. This census is the center of the comparison, because it is where the pattern repeats most exactly: a survey is commissioned, a cemetery is confirmed or left uncounted, and the results are published — or they are not. Four of the second-tier schools examined (Tulane, Maryland, Missouri, WashU) had no campus enslaved burial ground identified in any source read. Hollins’s Green Ridge is included with its type stated — a post-emancipation Black church cemetery containing formerly enslaved people — and is not counted as an antebellum enslaved cemetery.

The pattern, stated factually

Six of the fifteen grounds have had a GPR or equivalent geophysical survey (UAH, Clemson, Sewanee, Sweet Briar, Charleston, Richmond). Of those six, full results are public for two — Clemson (university-reported totals and policy) and Sweet Briar (Rainville 2008, itself partly inconclusive). The anchor case’s fact pattern — a survey was performed and its results were never published — recurs at Charleston (2026 GPR complete, report withheld pending SHPO release) and, in a variant, at Richmond (GPR inconclusive; no standalone report) and Sewanee (results at Reported grade). At the remaining grounds the recurring conditions are graves uncounted (LSU, Alabama, Emory, Georgetown), a location unknown (William & Mary), or remains moved over descendant objection (UGA). No institution in the census both completed a GPR survey and published a complete report of it other than Clemson and Sweet Briar.

The 1890 Land-Grant HBCUs — Treated Separately

Method & Sources

The five questions

A. Campus sited on former plantation land.
B. Enslaved labor documented in building or operating the campus.
C. Financial benefit from slavery — the institution sold enslaved people; endowment or gifts came from enslavers or slave-trade wealth; slave-sale proceeds.
D. Land dispute — descendant and heirs’ claims; burial-ground conflicts; Indigenous land under the Morrill Act land-grab findings; reparations demands.
E. Institutional response — official report, Universities Studying Slavery membership, memorial, apology, reparations or benefit fund.

The grades

Documented — established in a source read. Reported — carried by a secondary or advocacy source, not independently verified from a primary record. Not documented — not established in the sources read; that is a statement about the record, not proof of absence. Grades are never upgraded: when a later source changes a cell, the new finding is appended to the original wording in the research file in the form “ | CHUNK2: …” rather than written over it. That annotation, where it appears in the tables above, is the audit trail.

Principal sources

The Universities Studying Slavery consortium (hosted by the University of Virginia); Craig Steven Wilder, Ebony and Ivy: Race, Slavery, and the Troubled History of America’s Universities (2013); Robert Lee & Tristan Ahtone, Land-Grab Universities (High Country News, 2020) and the project’s downloadable database, queried directly for per-school Morrill acreage and principal; the institutional reports — Brown University, Slavery and Justice (2006); Harvard / Radcliffe Institute, Harvard and the Legacy of Slavery (2022); the University of Virginia President’s Commission on Slavery and the University; and Georgetown’s GU272 work and Working Group on Slavery, Memory, and Reconciliation (2016); and the Cardona–Vilsack letters of September 18, 2023, for the 1890 land-grant funding gaps.
