Open Data

The working databases behind Beyond Avalon — the source reads, name recoveries, and research leads — published as downloadable files. This is a research snapshot, not a finished archive: it mixes verified findings with working hypotheses, leads, and bounded negatives, and it says which is which.

Snapshot 2026-09-28 · redacted public release. Exported read-only from the live research spreadsheets; the live sheets keep changing. Names of living people and private email addresses have been removed — see what was redacted and why. Cite this snapshot by its date.

How to read this data — verified vs. hypothesis

No row may be cited as fact on its own authority. The verification level of each row is encoded in the table itself — read it before you quote anything:

  • DB1 Evidence Confidence — the primary signal. Free text, and the qualifier after the dash is the actual claim: High (manuscript read in full) is citable; Low — cover sheet read only; images unexamined is not.
  • DB1 H1–H8 hypothesis columns — each cell assesses what the row's source says about one of eight research hypotheses (Supports, Refutes, Mixed, Context, Unresolved). A row can support H3 and be unresolved on H5.
  • DB2 Evidence / Notes — verification status is stated in prose. There is no separate confidence column; the do-not-merge warnings in Notes are load-bearing. Names that look alike are not the same person until a source says so.
  • Leads Verification Status — nearly all values begin Unverified. Treat accordingly.

Thin-evidence convention: where evidence is thin, cells are left blank. A blank cell means "no data" — never "no," never "unknown person."

Avalon Research Database — 23 columns

#ColumnWhat it holds

2Document TypeCompiler-assigned label for the kind of thing the row is (e.g. Primary source, Census transcription, Finding Aid / Index Pointer (Lead)). The parenthetical qualifier is part of the meaning.
3SourceThe archive, publication, collection, or informant the row derives from.

7ConclusionsWhat the researcher concludes. Check Evidence Confidence before citing.
8QuestionsOpen questions the row raises. Research notes, not findings.
9CitationSource citation in a loose APA-like format.
10FileFilenames the row draws on — project-library filenames, not hyperlinks.
11–18H1–H8Hypothesis assessments (see codes below).
19Evidence TypeKind of evidence (e.g. Chancery manuscript — firsthand read of page images). The dash-qualifier carries the verification claim.
20Thesis CategoryMixed-use: status-like values (Supports, Verified findings, Unresolved, Lead, Context) or topic labels. Read each value literally.
21NotesWorking notes: transcription caveats, do-not-merge warnings, read-level notes.
22Evidence ConfidenceThe row's verification level in the researcher's own words. The most important column for judging whether a row is citable.
23Source FingerprintResearcher-assigned provenance slug for the source object.

H1–H8 hypothesis codes

CodeHypothesisWhat counts
H1Institutional knowledge / erasureInstitutional notice, memorialization, omission, disturbance, or erasure
H2Legal-archival erasureLaw, property, probate, bureaucracy, archival naming, archival silences
H3Black Jones lineageIdentifies, distinguishes, or connects Black Jones individuals and families
H4Shandy parentageBears on Shandy's identity, mother, age, father, or descendants
H5APJ children hypothesisBears on whether Alexander P. Jones fathered children with an enslaved woman
H6Plantation kinship / economyJones-Donnell-Haywood-Perkins kinship, landholding, plantation commerce, transatlantic business
H7Black agency / post-emancipationEnslaved or freedpeople's action, family formation, claims, work, politics, education, community life
H8Patrol / KKK / racial violenceSlave patrols, KKK, racial terror, coercion, organized white violence

Cell values: Supports (affirmative evidence; strength depends on source quality) · Refutes (contradicts a proposed connection) · Mixed · Context (background, not proof) · Unresolved (bears on the question, doesn't decide it). Blank means not assessed. A few cells carry legacy Yes/TRUE values whose exact meaning is unconfirmed — do not treat them as Supports without checking.

What was redacted and why

This release is redacted. The project's working databases name living people — descendants who gave interviews, family correspondents, local historians, and staff who answered research requests. Interview participation is not publication consent, so until each person opts in, they appear here as [REDACTED — living person].

Also removed: one personal email address, five staff email addresses used in research correspondence, and the researcher's own university email address. Public reference-desk addresses (archives, special collections) are kept. Historical figures — including the 1950s Willie Jones family at the center of the land-reclamation research, who appear in public court records and news reporting — are named as the sources name them.

Where a redaction would have removed a historically significant public record, the name is kept: redaction targets living private individuals, not the public record. If you are named in this data and want your name restored or removed, use the contact link below.

Data-quality caveats

  • No single status column exists yet. Verification level must be read from Evidence Confidence (DB1), the H-column codes (DB1), or prose in Evidence/Notes (DB2).
  • Free-text coded columns. Document Type, Evidence Type, Thesis Category, and Evidence Confidence are researcher-assigned free text, not controlled vocabularies. Identical strings were not necessarily applied under identical criteria.
  • Blank = no data. Never "no," never "unknown person," never filled by inference.
  • Method rows. DB2 contains negative-control and method rows with no person; the Leads tab is unverified by definition.
  • Snapshot, not a feed. Dated 2026-09-28. The live sheets keep changing; cite the snapshot date.
