Priscilla Jones, free 20 years, sails for Liberia — Madison County’s documented 1852 emigration Documented

A verified read of Morris Raymond Boucher’s 1950 dissertation (The Free Negro in Alabama Prior to 1860, State University of Iowa) documents a Madison County emancipation-to-Liberia migration: Priscilla Jones, emancipated by John McConnell of Madison County, had been free for over twenty years when she sailed for Liberia in December 1852 from Savannah, Georgia, with six freeborn children and grandchildren. She was one of seven people McConnell had freed, sailing in a group of fifteen — eight emancipated slaves and seven free persons of color. Sixteen Alabama emigrants sailed that year in all (Alabama ninth among the states); Squire Grant, a free man of Glenville, had sailed separately in June. The dissertation cites American Colonization Society reports and the African Repository.

No record connects Priscilla Jones to the Lewellen Jones family of Madison County, and none is claimed here: a shared surname is not kinship. She is also not merged with Priscilla Drake of the Drake-attribution question, nor with the “Priscilla Jones” enumerated among free people of color in Boucher’s Marshall County appendix census table — three separate identities until direct evidence says otherwise.
