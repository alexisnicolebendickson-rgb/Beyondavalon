Shandy Wesley Jones

Free Black barber and Reconstruction-era legislator from Tuscaloosa County, Alabama — born in Madison County, moved south by 1837, and elected to the state house in 1868.

DRAFT — not for publication. Staged for review September 2026. Nothing on this page goes live without the researcher’s word. Evidence levels: Documented Under investigation Unresolved
How to read this page. Published scholarship treats the Madison County Shandy and the Tuscaloosa Shandy as one man — Shandy Wesley Jones. He is not merged with Columbus Jones’s deceased brother Shandy, whom the 1867 bank register lists as already dead; the legislator was alive in 1868. Documented

Life in brief

Shandy Wesley Jones was born in Madison County, Alabama — scholarship gives 1816–1886 — and moved to Tuscaloosa by 1837, where he built a barbershop into the free Black community’s most visible institution. Documented He served as Tuscaloosa County’s representative in the Alabama legislature from 1868 to 1870. Documented Scholarship associates him with the Jones network through family oral history and a professional genealogy, while flagging the age problem at its center — but no document names his father. Unresolved

Tuscaloosa household Documented

The 1860 federal census places Shandy’s family in Tuscaloosa, next door to Columbus Jones’s household in a mostly white neighborhood; both men are listed as barbers or blacksmiths (Hoksbergen & DeNeefe, citing the 1860 census).

1867

Constitutional convention delegate Documented

Delegate to Alabama’s 1867 constitutional convention — one of the two legislators this project tracks (the other being Columbus Jones).

1868–1870

Tuscaloosa County representative Documented

Served in the Alabama legislature from Tuscaloosa County. While the Madison County bank register of October 1867 lists a brother “Shandy” of Columbus as deceased, Shandy Wesley Jones was alive and legislating in 1868 — the two are not merged.

The Jones-network link

Hoksbergen and DeNeefe report family oral history and a professional genealogy making Columbus and Reuben the half-brothers of Shandy, associating Eliza with Elizabeth, the woman manumitted in 1820 — while flagging the age problem: Elizabeth would have been about 47 at Columbus’s birth, about 53 at Reuben’s, with younger siblings born as late as 1843. What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Shandy Wesley Jones

  • No document names his father. The Jones-network link rests on oral history and a professional genealogy, not on a primary record.
  • The dates and occupation given in earlier drafts of this project — 1812–1874, Huntsville barber — are unsupported; scholarship gives 1816–1886, Tuscaloosa barber. The unsupported figures are not repeated here.
  • His relationship to the Madison County Jones family, if any, is unresolved — including the claimed half-brother link to Columbus and Reuben.
