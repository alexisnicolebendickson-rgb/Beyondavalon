Anthony & Mary Jones

Anthony Jones filed Southern Claims Commission Claim No. 3,137 in 1871 — and signed it in his own hand, not with an X-mark. Mary Jones (1812–1865) lies in the Jones (Mary) Cemetery. Whether they were husband and wife is unconfirmed.

How to read this page. Every claim names its source. Where the record is silent, this page says so instead of filling the gap. Anthony and Mary share a page because the cemetery and the surname put them near each other — not because any document calls them husband and wife. That relationship is unconfirmed. Under investigation

Life in brief

Anthony Jones appears in Madison County in the 1860s and 1870s: among the tenants and purchasers settling the estate of Alexander P. Jones — the Madison County planter enumerated with ninety-six enslaved people in 1860 — in 1866–67, and in the Southern Claims Commission files in 1871. Documented His SCC filing, Claim No. 3,137, was barred and disallowed — but the filing itself survives, and on it Anthony Jones signed his own name rather than making an X-mark. Documented For a Black man in Reconstruction Alabama, a signature on a federal form is not a small thing; it is evidence of literacy the archive almost never records, and of a man who understood what a written claim was. Documented He is also recorded in the Madison County cemetery surveys of 1970 and 2014. Documented

Mary Jones (1812–1865) is buried in the Jones (Mary) Cemetery in Madison County. Documented She died the year the war ended. The name, the dates, and the ground place her in Anthony’s world — but no document names her as his wife, and this page does not either. Under investigation

Southern Claims Commission, Claim No. 3,137 Documented

Anthony Jones files a claim with the Southern Claims Commission — the federal body that compensated Southern Unionists for property taken by the Union army. The claim was barred and disallowed. On the 1871 filing, he signs his own name rather than making an X-mark: direct evidence of literacy.

What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Anthony & Mary Jones

  • No record names Anthony Jones’s enslaver, his birth date, or his death date. He enters the written record as a free man making a federal claim.
  • The full text of SCC Claim No. 3,137 has not yet been read firsthand — the file is the sharpest next source, and it may name family, property, and witnesses.
  • No document confirms Mary Jones as Anthony’s wife. She shares his surname, his county, and his cemetery ground — that is the whole of the evidence, and it is not enough.
  • No record connects Anthony Jones to the Avalon Jones network by kinship. He stands in the network’s geography and its records, and no further.
  • Three different Anthonys appear in this project’s records — the 1805 deed-of-gift Anthony, Anthony Walker, and this man. The archive reuses names; this page does not reuse people.
