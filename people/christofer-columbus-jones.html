Christofer Columbus Jones

1872–1939. Grandson of Columbus Jones, documented at every step from the 1880 census to 1930 — the descendant page that proves the family’s paper trail survived.

How to read this page. Every claim names its source. Where the record is silent, this page says so instead of filling the gap. The archive reuses the name Columbus Jones for at least three different men — this page is about the youngest of them, Christofer Columbus Jones (1872–1939), and keeps the other two firewalled below. Documented

Life in brief

Christofer Columbus Jones was born in 1872 and died in 1939 — a Black man of Madison County, Alabama, and the grandson of Columbus Jones (1827–1869), the blacksmith, delegate to the 1867 constitutional convention, and Madison County’s state representative in 1868, through Columbus’s son Paul Jones Sr. (1847–1918). Documented He is one of the best-documented people in the project’s whole family tree: the federal census finds him in 1880, 1910, 1920, and 1930; Madison County holds his full two-page 1925 marriage license and certificate; and the record adds cemetery records, Social Security applications, and newspaper index entries. Documented For a Black family in Alabama, an unbroken paper trail across five decades is the exception — this page exists because Christofer is the exception, and because his records carry his grandfather’s line into the twentieth century.

His daughter Susan Hatfield married Reuben’s nephew — a documented cross-cousin marriage knitting the Columbus and Reuben Jones lines together, the two brothers’ descendants choosing each other a generation later. Documented

What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Christofer Columbus Jones

  • For all the census appearances, no record in the project yet gives Christofer’s occupation, his landholding, or his own words about his family. The paper trail proves he was counted; it does not yet say how he lived.
  • The 1900 census — the decade of his marriage-age years — has not yet been pulled for him. The 1925 marriage record exists, but the years between 1880 and 1910 are thin.
  • The newspaper index entries have not yet been read in full. They may name him in community life, or they may be passing mentions — either way, the reading is owed.
  • Three different men named Columbus Jones appear in this project’s records. This page is the youngest; the archive’s habit of reusing names is the reason the do-not-merge cards exist.
