Fortune

An enslaved man at Seclusion plantation in Lawrence County, Alabama — personal servant and coach driver to the Donnell family. He died in February 1859, and his enslaver raised an eight-foot granite stone over him. The stone speaks; Fortune’s own words do not survive.

How to read this page. Every claim names its source. Where the record is silent, this page says so instead of filling the gap. Almost everything known about Fortune comes from the people who enslaved him — this page says what they said, and marks the boundary where their words end. Documented

Life in brief

Fortune was enslaved at Seclusion, the Donnell family’s plantation in Lawrence County, Alabama — the family whose papers sit at the center of this project’s Tennessee State Library & Archives pull. Documented He served as personal servant and coach driver. Documented He died in February 1859. Documented His enslaver had an eight-foot granite tombstone raised over his grave, inscribed: “Fortune… Died February, 1859… To the virtues and excellencies of a faithful [servant] this testimony is erected by his mas[ter].” Documented

The stone is a strange kind of evidence. Eight feet of granite is not what enslaved people usually got — it marks a man his enslaver wanted remembered, on the enslaver’s terms. “Faithful servant” is the language of the people who owned him, not of Fortune himself. This page keeps both truths in view: the stone proves he lived and was valued, and it proves nothing about what Fortune thought of the arrangement. Documented

What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Fortune

  • No record gives Fortune’s birth date, birthplace, or age at death. He enters the written record only at its end.
  • No record names Fortune’s family — no parents, no spouse, no children. The eight-foot stone names no one but him.
  • Everything written about Fortune was written by the people who enslaved him or their descendants. “Faithful servant” is their language, and this page does not mistake it for his.
  • No record says what Fortune thought of his position, his enslaver, or the stone. His own words do not survive.
  • A separate “Fortune Donnell” burial under a concrete headstone has been reported, but the source has not been read firsthand — the two are kept separate until it is.
  • The Donnell family papers at TSLA (THS 296) may hold more on Fortune and on Seclusion’s enslaved people generally; that pull is still in progress.
