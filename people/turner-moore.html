Counter-archive · Person page

Turner Moore

Named in the Freedman’s Bank record as the father of Columbus Jones, Reconstruction-era legislator — and almost nothing else. No document names the white Moore who enslaved him.

How to read this page. Turner Moore is the most thinly documented person in this directory — he appears in exactly one record. Everything below is what that record says, plus what the archive does not give. He is not merged with Turner Anderson, with Columbus’s brother Turner Jones, or with the unresolved 1866 contract signer. Unresolved

Life in brief

Turner Moore is named as the father of Columbus Jones in Columbus’s Freedman’s Bank signature register, opened October 2, 1867: “father Turner Moore d Mar 1867 at son’s home.” Documented He died in March 1867, at his son’s home, roughly three miles northwest of Huntsville at the end of Athens Pike. Documented No other record names him — not the census, not the voter registers, not the Freedman’s Bureau contracts. Unresolved The white Moore who enslaved him has not been identified; the evidence that Columbus’s family and the Moore plantations were connected is circumstantial, and forced separation by sale remains unsupported. Unresolved

Records

Before 1867

Enslaved — by whom, unknown Unresolved

No pre-1867 record names Turner Moore. No record identifies the white Moore who enslaved him. A candidate enslaver is Benjamin T. Moore (1813–1878), the Madison County lawyer and nephew of Governor Gabriel Moore who took over his uncle’s estate in the 1830s — but no document ties Turner to Benjamin, and the identification is not proven.

March 1867

Death, at his son’s home Documented

Columbus’s bank register: “father Turner Moore d Mar 1867 at son’s home.” His son’s home stood about three miles northwest of Huntsville at the end of Athens Pike. No burial record has been found.

October 2, 1867

Named as Columbus’s father Documented

Registers of Signatures of Depositors, Freedman’s Savings and Trust Company, Huntsville branch (NARA M816, Roll 1) — Columbus Jones entry. The register’s father field reads: “Turner Moore d Mar 1867 at son’s home.”

Kinship

Son

Columbus Jones

The only source for this relationship is Columbus’s own bank register. Documented

Do not merge with

Turner Anderson · Turner Jones · the 1866 signer

Turner Anderson (the Louisa County name) is a different man. Turner Jones, Columbus’s brother, was alive in 1867 and cannot be Columbus’s father. The “Turner Moore” who signed a Freedman’s Bureau contract in 1866 is unassigned and unidentified. Unresolved

Places

Northwest of Huntsville Columbus’s home at the end of Athens Pike, about three miles out — where Turner Moore died in March 1867. View on the Plantations Map →
The Moore plantations The white Moores’ holdings — Governor Gabriel Moore’s estate and Benjamin T. Moore’s land — sat within the Madison County plantation belt. No document places Turner on any Moore holding. Unresolved

What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Turner Moore

  • No record identifies the white Moore who enslaved Turner. Benjamin T. Moore is a candidate enslaver, not a proven one.
  • Forced separation by sale — the mechanism by which Turner came to be separated from his son — remains unsupported. The evidence slightly favors a neighboring-plantations or “abroad marriage” structure, but the mechanism is unknown.
  • No census, voter register, or Bureau record names Turner Moore. The 1867 voter registers contain no “Turner Moore,” consistent with his March 1867 death.
  • The unresolved 1866 Freedman’s Bureau contract signer “Turner Moore” is kept unassigned — not merged with Turner here, not merged with Turner Jones.
  • No burial record, no age, no birthplace, no occupation. He exists in the archive as a name in his son’s hand.

Sources

  1. Registers of Signatures of Depositors in Branches of the Freedman’s Savings and Trust Company, 1865–1874, Huntsville, Alabama (National Archives Microfilm Publication M816, Roll 1) — Columbus Jones entry, October 2, 1867; extracted in Fred C. Rathbun, Names from Huntsville, Alabama, 1865–1869.
  2. Alabama Department of Archives and History, 1867 Voter Registration Books, Madison County vols. 1–2 — no “Turner Moore” entry.
  3. Ben Hoksbergen and Jane DeNeefe, “Keeping Up with the Joneses: Tracing the Lives and Legacies of Two Reconstruction-Era Legislators from Madison County,” Alabama Review 79, no. 3 (July 2026).
