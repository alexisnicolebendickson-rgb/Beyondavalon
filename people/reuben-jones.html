Reuben Jones

Reconstruction-era legislator and Huntsville landowner from Madison County, Alabama — a blacksmith who bought plantation land in January 1871 and served in the state house in 1872–73. Born about 1833 in Madison County.

How to read this page. Reuben’s own bank register names his father as Simon and his mother as Eliza Jones — not Turner Moore. That stands even though his brother Columbus’s register names Turner Moore as father. The two records are kept side by side without forcing them to agree. Documented

Life in brief

Reuben Jones was born about 1833 in Madison County, Alabama, and worked as a blacksmith. Documented In January 1871 he bought land parceled out of the Drake holdings — plantation land sold, not granted, to a freedman (Madison County Deed Books TT and UU). Documented He served as Madison County’s representative in the Alabama legislature in 1872–73, and became a major Huntsville landowner and subdivider of the Chelsea enclave. Documented His Freedman’s Bank account, opened December 24, 1872, names his father as Simon and his mother as Eliza Jones. Documented He died October 5, 1898. Documented

Records

c. 1833

Born, Madison County, Alabama Documented

Ages reported in federal censuses and his Freedman’s Bank signature register place his birth around 1833. No birth record names him.

1860

Tuscaloosa household Documented

The 1860 federal census for Tuscaloosa lists Reuben with Jane (19) and daughter Emily (2); he is listed as a blacksmith (Hoksbergen & DeNeefe, citing the 1860 census).

June 24, 1867

Voter registration, Madison County Under investigation

“Jones Ruben” — the clerk’s spelling — registered the same day and precinct as Columbus Jones and Turner Jones, Madison County Precinct 1, Oath Book 1, p. 94. Consistent with the bank record’s brother Reuben; identity is suggestive, not proven.

1869–1870

School lease Documented

Reuben leased land to William Hooper Councill’s freedmen’s school in 1869–70 — the lease that ties the Jones family’s land to the founding of the school that became Alabama A&M.

January 1871

Land purchase from the Drakes Documented

Reuben bought land in January 1871 (Madison County Deed Books TT, p. 488 and UU, p. 350 — the latter two acres south of the former). The grantors were the Drakes, who owned the plantation from about 1868 to 1871: plantation land parceled to a freedman by sale. On November 2, 1872, Reuben and his wife Mollie transferred land to Lucy Thompson (Deed Book TT, p. 489) — an intra-family transfer.

1870

Wife Jane dies Documented

Reuben’s wife Jane died in June 1870 (1870 federal census, Madison County, p. 13).

1872–1873

State legislator Documented

Madison County representative in the Alabama legislature.

December 24, 1872

Freedman’s Bank account opened Documented

Registers of Signatures of Depositors, Freedman’s Savings and Trust Company, Huntsville branch (NARA M816, Roll 1), account 1406. Reuben names his father as Simon and his mother as Eliza Jones — not Turner Moore. He married Mollie Richardson in 1872; she died in 1888.

1889

Third marriage Documented

Married Malvina “Vinie” Harrison.

October 5, 1898

Death Documented

Died October 5, 1898.

Kinship

Father

Simon

Named in Reuben’s own bank register. Kept separate from Turner Moore — no merge. Documented

Mother

Eliza Jones

Named in Reuben’s bank register. Kept separate from Elizabeth, the woman manumitted in 1820. Documented

Brothers

Columbus Jones, Turner Jones

Named in Columbus’s bank register. Note: Columbus’s register names their father as Turner Moore; Reuben’s names Simon. The records disagree, and this page does not resolve the disagreement. Unresolved

Wives

Jane; Mollie Richardson; Malvina “Vinie” Harrison

Jane died June 1870. Mollie Richardson, married 1872, died 1888. Malvina Harrison, married 1889. Documented

Daughter

Emily

Aged two in the 1860 Tuscaloosa census, with Reuben and Jane. Documented

Places

Madison County, Alabama Birthplace (~1833); the January 1871 Drake land purchases; the Chelsea enclave he later subdivided. View on the Plantations Map →

What the record doesn’t say

The silence ledger for Reuben

  • No record names Reuben before 1860. His enslaver — if he was enslaved, and by whom — is undocumented.
  • His bank account (No. 1406, December 24, 1872) has not been verified firsthand from the M816 manuscript; the free transcription source reaches only March 1870. The father-named-Simon reading rests on the later extraction and needs the manuscript check.
  • An earlier research pass recorded Reuben’s father as Turner Moore, matching Columbus’s register. The two readings conflict; the conflict is unresolved and is stated here rather than hidden.
  • No record identifies Simon — no surname, no age, no status, no owner.
  • The deed texts for the January 1871 purchases (Books TT and UU) are recorded but the legal descriptions remain unpinned; boundary work is provisional.
