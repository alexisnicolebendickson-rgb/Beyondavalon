People

The people the project has traced through the Alabama records — legislators, landowners, a barber, a coach driver, a freed family, and the women the archive barely names. Each page carries its evidence and its silences side by side.

c. 1780 – after 1820

Elizabeth

Manumitted by a special Alabama legislative act in 1820, along with Evelina, Ann, and Shandy. The researcher’s only maternal lead — and the maternity question stays open.

Read her page → c. 1827 – January 1869

Columbus Jones

Blacksmith, delegate to the 1867 constitutional convention, and Madison County’s state representative in 1868. His bank register names his whole family.

Read his page → c. 1833 – October 5, 1898

Reuben Jones

Blacksmith, state legislator 1872–73, and Huntsville landowner who bought plantation land in January 1871. His bank account names a different father than his brother’s.

Read his page → ? – March 1867

Turner Moore

Named as Columbus Jones’s father — and almost nothing else. No record names the white Moore who enslaved him.

Read his page → c. 1816 – 1886

Shandy Wesley Jones

Free Black barber of Tuscaloosa and its Reconstruction legislator, 1868–70. Scholarship treats his Madison County and Tuscaloosa records as one life.

Read his page → ? – after 1867

Tomietta Jones

Columbus’s wife, named once in his bank register and then lost. The 1870 census is where the search for her begins.

Read her page → 1890 – 1972

Dora Jones

Born in Athens, Alabama to formerly enslaved parents; held in unpaid domestic labor for nearly forty years until her case broke in 1947. Her tie to the Avalon Jones network is unproven — she stands here as a lead and a twentieth-century continuity story.

Read her page → ? – after 1871

Anthony & Mary Jones

He signed his own name on an 1871 Southern Claims Commission filing; she lies in the Jones (Mary) Cemetery. Whether they were husband and wife is unconfirmed.

Read their page → ? – February 1859

Fortune

Enslaved at Seclusion plantation in Lawrence County — personal servant and coach driver, buried under an eight-foot granite stone raised by his enslaver.

Read his page → ? – after 1876

Nancy, Marshall & Zachariah Jones

A freed family of the APJ plantation: Nancy’s 1876 deposition describes farming eighty acres of corn after emancipation; Marshall was the plantation’s “head man”; their likely son Zachariah was denied his federal claim because slavery had denied him literacy.

Read their page → ? – after 1958

Willie (Fay) Jones

The man who lost the well: in 1954 the City of Huntsville condemned the well site on his family’s Athens Pike land, and in 1958 he sold the parcel. His siblings carry the land-reclamation fight today.

Read his page → 1872 – 1939

Christofer Columbus Jones

Grandson of Columbus Jones, documented at every step from the 1880 census to 1930 — the descendant page that proves the family’s paper trail survived.
